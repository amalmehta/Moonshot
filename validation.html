Validation

Checked against the real thing

This project is a reimplementation in the spirit of a paper study: every model is derived from first principles, then compared with a published figure. Each row is computed live in your browser by the same code the pages use, and the same rows run as automated tests (npm test). The tolerance is the error we consider honest for that model, and the note says what the model leaves out.

Method

  • Inputs are public: SpaceX vehicle pages and user’s guide, FAA/FCC/NASA documents, and (marked “est.”) widely cited estimates where SpaceX hasn’t published a number.
  • No fitting to the answer. The only fitted parameters are the effective exhaust-gas γ and molar mass, each fitted to one engine (Merlin 1D, Raptor 2) and then used to predict the others. The ascent pitch-kick angle was chosen to maximize payload, as a real trajectory designer would, not to match 22.8 t.
  • The landing reserve is derived too. The propellant Falcon 9 keeps for recovery comes from its entry and landing burns, not from the payload it produces.
  • Only checks that could fail against the real world are listed. Maths self-checks (Kalman, redundancy and guidance identities) and input look-ups are unit tests instead.
  • Tolerances reflect model fidelity: tight (≤ 1 %) for exact physics such as standard-atmosphere tables and Kepler periods; loose (10–25 %) where the inputs themselves are estimates (stage masses, costs).
